01 — The screens

Everything happens on one screen

There is no tab bar and no home screen. The map is always underneath, the chrome floats over it as glass, and every sheet is one tap from driving. Controls are sized for a thumb on a bracket-mounted phone, not a fingertip on a desk.

02 — The decision

Is it on my road, or just near me?

Everything turns on this. A police car a kilometre ahead on the road you are driving is worth knowing about now. One a kilometre away on some other road is not — you may never go near it. Treating them the same either floods you with irrelevance or warns you far too late about the one that counts.

On the route
Best answer of the four, and the only one that survives a bend. Within 45 m of the route line, measured along the road — so a police car around a sweeping curve, 60° off your compass heading, is still correctly a kilometre up your own road.
The corridor
Without a route: 40 m either side alongside you, widening by 2 cm per metre to absorb heading noise, capped at 90 m before it can reach the next street. Australian blocks put parallel roads further apart than that, which is what makes the cap work.
The cone
In front, but off the corridor — a service road, a slip lane, a bend the corridor could not follow. Gets the ordinary lead rather than the long one.
The radius
Where geometry gives up. Inside it, direction stops mattering: an intersection you are approaching sits at nearly 90°, and something just around a corner is off in an arbitrary direction.
Sideways is radius-only
A long lead time never reaches sideways. Otherwise going fast would warn you about a police car half a kilometre off your route — but your speed says nothing about how soon you reach something beside you.

Why Android does not blur

MapLibre draws the map into a SurfaceView, which the system composites on its own layer underneath the app window. The Compose hierarchy never holds those pixels.

So the backdrop-blur libraries — Haze, Cloudy, Imla — which work by capturing the Compose root into a texture, capture a hole where the map is. They do not run slowly over a map. They cannot see it.

The one way to make it blurrable is running MapLibre in texture mode, which costs a full-screen copy every frame on the one surface that never stops redrawing. Paying that so a panel edge can be frosted is a bad trade in an app whose job is to keep working at 100 km/h.

Painted glass — a translucent base, a vertical gradient, a rim that is bright where light enters and catches again on the far edge — costs a handful of draw calls, no readback and no extra render pass. Over a dark map, at arm's length, in a car: you will not be able to tell.

04 — Behaviour

What the interface does when you are not looking at it

Most of the design is in what it refuses to do. An app that talks constantly is one you learn to ignore, and that is worse than silence, because you stop hearing the warning that mattered.

Warning cone
Narrows with speed — 70° at a standstill, 25° at 100 km/h. At speed, anything 40° off your nose is a different road.
Warning distance
A lead time, not a distance: 25 s for a camera, 35 s for a closure, converted to metres at your current speed. 500 m is ten seconds of highway and a full minute in traffic.
Other carriageway
A camera that names the direction it faces stays silent unless your heading is within 60° of it.
Half-believed reports
Below 0.5 confidence, a report chimes instead of speaking. Below 0.3 it is not shown at all. One stranger's single tap does not get a voice.
Repeats
Ten-minute cooldown per hazard, six seconds between any two announcements, and anything you have passed is retired for good rather than merely cooled down.
Parked
Stationary for over a minute and it goes completely silent. Parked next to a camera should not chirp at you.
Turn instructions
Spoken twice at most — once around 200–600 m out, once inside 60 m. Never queued: a backlog of instructions is worse than none, because the one being read is no longer the one you are approaching.
Losing signal
Cameras live on the phone, so it degrades to cameras-only rather than to nothing, and says so on screen.
Screen off
Warnings keep coming. Android runs a foreground service with a visible notification; iOS uses the location background mode.
